Highlight Protection
{{ form_config.HIGHLIGHT_PROTECTION__ENABLE.label.text }}

Limit the largest connected bright patches and lift darker areas using your existing image settings. Applies to day, night and moon mode. Disable and reload to restore normal ADU control and processing.

Shadow lift and highlight gamma engage smoothly when clipping exceeds either lower limit or blocks a needed exposure increase. They remain active while exposure recovers, then fade back to ordinary rendering once both patches have clear headroom and ordinary brightness or the achievable exposure/gain ceiling is reached. A clear sky that never needs protection keeps normal rendering. Set highlight gamma to 0 to keep normal gamma, or 1 for no gamma correction. Day/night selection follows the existing color profile, including Use Night Color Settings. Disabling highlight protection immediately restores normal processing. Your normal gamma values are kept unchanged.

Areas are percentages of the Detection Mask, or the ADU ROI/FoV when no mask is set. All-channel counts white clipping; any-channel includes it and coloured clipping. Each measures its largest connected patch. For monochrome images both measure the same patch. Target 0.8 with deviation 0.2 holds between 0.6% and 1.0%. Either upper limit reduces exposure; both lower limits allow recovery toward the ordinary ADU target. Requests use the selected exposure/gain mode.

The clipping threshold is measured before dark calibration and stretching, as a percentage of the configured image bit depth's full scale. Start at 99%. Maximum shadow lift uses calibrated brightness and limits how far it may fall below the ordinary ADU target; 2 stops permits up to 4× compensation. Exposure/gain limits or this lift limit can prevent meeting the highlight targets. Every usable frame is evaluated. Clipping corrections taper smoothly to zero at the upper limits, with reductions capped at 20%. Correction strength grows gradually as camera settings take effect. Brightness recovery stays capped at 10%. When enabled, capture adjustments are sent before stacking and image enhancement to reduce delay. When disabled, ordinary exposure control keeps its original timing and behavior. Purple-frame repair is not required. When enabled, detected purple frames hold exposure/gain; repaired images retain the last trusted protection state. Existing save and reload behavior is unchanged.

Requires an interface that can command camera exposure. Passive INDI, downloaded images and read-only exposure controls automatically use normal processing, with a log entry and notification. Discrete ISO and microsecond exposure steps may exceed the proportional adjustment limits when no smaller hardware step is available.
